Privacy at MealLog

Updated October 20, 2026

Updated October 20, 2026. Controller: Monish Venkata Naga Sai Nallamilli. Contact: appsupport@duck.com.

MealLog is a food journal for iPhone and iPad. You can use a guest journal without creating an account. We do not sell your data, use advertising/tracking SDKs, or send journal text or photos for AI analysis.

Your device

Meal descriptions, dates/times, reference nutrition, optional photos, drafts and preferences are stored on your device. Guest journals are not uploaded. Exports are created locally and sent only to the destination you choose. Protect exported files and any recovery key you keep separately.

Apple sign-in and automatic account recovery

Signing in with Apple enables automatic account storage of saved meals. The sign-in screen asks for your consent to encrypted storage of your food journal in the United States. You may use a guest journal instead. Apple authenticates you and may share a relay or other email address. The app stores the account identifier, email and session credentials in Keychain. Supabase processes account identifiers, authentication email and ordinary connection/security metadata to authenticate and protect your account.

Saved meal records are encrypted on your device with AES-GCM before upload to Supabase. The database stores ciphertext, opaque meal/account identifiers and revision/update metadata. Food descriptions, meal dates and reference nutrients are not readable database fields. The service cannot decrypt journal content from stored ciphertext alone. Encryption does not make account-linked data anonymous or remove your privacy rights. Photos, drafts and preferences remain local.

A random encryption key, containing no meal content, can synchronize through iCloud Keychain. Your saved meals can return after reinstall or on another device when you sign in with the same Apple account and your key is available. If Keychain recovery is unavailable, a separately kept verified recovery key may be necessary. We cannot recover a lost encryption key. Meal records do not use CloudKit. Internet access and available service capacity are required; iOS controls background execution, so updates are not guaranteed while the app is closed.

You can withdraw future account uploads in Account → Account privacy & recovery → Save meals to my account. This pauses new uploads on that device and does not erase an existing account copy. Export or delete your account separately if desired. Reconnecting with Apple enables account storage again with the disclosure shown at sign-in.

Family and friends

Family progress is optional. The first inviter creates a family and becomes manager. Accepting an invitation shares seven days of your daily meal counts and days logged with all current family members and lets you see their progress. Food names, exact meal times, calories, nutrients, quality scores, photos and email are not shared. Each account can belong to one family with at most 20 members. Neutral Member N labels are used; an optional nickname set for a member stays on your device and is not sent to the service or other members.

Progress is encrypted separately for each recipient on-device. The service stores ciphertext, public agreement keys, opaque account/member/group identifiers, roles, invitation hashes, acceptance/expiry and update metadata. Private decryption/agreement keys are not uploaded. Invitations contain a secret one-use token rather than your journal key, expire after seven days, and should be sent privately only to intended recipients. Anyone holding an unused invitation could accept it. Opening a link alone does not join or share anything; the recipient must sign in and accept. No contacts upload or automatic clipboard reading occurs.

You can leave with one tap; the manager can remove a member. Server access is revoked and that membership's incoming/outgoing progress rows are removed. Current app views revalidate membership before showing updates, refresh while visible, and hide unavailable/backgrounded progress. Updates need internet and sender activity; they are not instant monitoring. Previously saved external copies cannot be recalled. If the manager leaves, the longest-standing remaining member becomes manager; if everyone leaves, the group is removed. Reports use the user-reviewed support-email flow below.

Earlier versions used one-way connections and encrypted display names/optional quality summaries. Existing encrypted legacy vaults may remain with the account until deletion; creating or joining a new family closes that account's old one-way connections. No previous one-way invitation is silently converted into mutual sharing. There is no public feed, custom shared profile text, messaging or health monitoring.

Notifications, widgets and app sharing

Optional reminders are local notifications with generic meal-category messages. Times, repeat days and sound choices stay on the device. Turn reminders off to cancel pending reminders. iOS Focus, notification settings and background limits may affect delivery.

Quick-log widgets open a meal form without saving automatically. Daily count widgets are off by default per local journal. If enabled, only today's four category counts and freshness date/time-zone metadata enter a local App Group cache excluded from backups. Food names, nutrients, photos, account IDs and credentials are not included. The widget does not contact a server. Anyone who can see your widget may see enabled counts. Signing out clears its cache; iOS controls when an already displayed snapshot refreshes.

Sharing the app sends only its listing or beta invitation link through the destination you choose, never your journal.

Barcode providers and nutrition

Scanning a packaged-food barcode sends the product code to Open Food Facts for public product/nutrition details. It and network providers may receive ordinary connection information, such as IP address and the app's public User-Agent. Meal photos and journal records are not sent to this provider. Local reference searches use a bundled USDA catalog. External links follow the destination's privacy policy.

Nutrition and quality labels are informational estimates per 100 g or 100 mL, not medical advice or a measure of consumed amounts. Missing or conflicting data may leave a food unrated. Confirm product/preparation matches and consult a qualified professional for dietary or medical decisions.

Retention, deletion and your rights

Local journals remain until you erase them, delete the account on that device, or remove the app, subject to your device's backup settings. Account records remain until you delete your account. Unused invitations expire after seven days; revocation removes future sharing access.

You can export saved meals and initiate permanent account deletion in MealLog → Account → Delete account. After your verified request, the hosted Auth identity and associated journal/sharing rows are hard-deleted. The app then removes that account's local journal, drafts, photo files and encryption keys on the requesting device. Cleanup resumes on launch if interrupted. Other devices and exports may retain local copies. Sign-out alone does not delete an account. Apple's separate authorization can be removed in Apple Account → Sign-In & Security → Sign in with Apple → MealLog → Delete; the app provides this instruction after deletion. The native-only service does not retain a revocable Apple refresh/access token.

Service security logs, provider backups and support correspondence may remain for their applicable security, operational or legal retention periods under provider policies; database deletion does not promise immediate removal from every provider log or an exported copy. Contact us for access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, consent withdrawal or questions about retention. Depending on your location, you may complain to your data protection authority. We may need to verify an account request without asking for your journal encryption key.

Providers and international processing

Apple provides authentication and optional key-only iCloud Keychain recovery. Supabase provides account and encrypted journal/family storage in US East. Open Food Facts provides barcode results. The app uses their services for the functions described above. See Apple privacy, Supabase privacy, Supabase data processing terms and Open Food Facts privacy.

Where consent is required for processing a food journal that may reveal health-related information, we request it at Apple sign-in; family sharing is a separate voluntary choice. Account and security metadata also support the service you request and abuse prevention, as allowed by applicable law. US hosting involves international processing for people outside the US. Free-tier hosting and encryption do not by themselves establish legal compliance; distribution must use applicable processor/transfer safeguards. We will update this policy when the service or providers materially change.

Reporting a problem

Open the MealLog menu → Report a problem. Write what happened and review the email draft before sending it to appsupport@duck.com. No journal, photos, credentials or logs are attached automatically. App/iOS version is optional and off by default. Sending a report shares your email sender address and whatever you choose to write with support and email providers. Do not include passwords or recovery keys. If no email app is available, copy the support address and use your email service. Support correspondence is used to respond and investigate issues; contact support for privacy requests.
